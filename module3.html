Introduction

Your First Line of Defence

Weak or reused passwords are one of the leading causes of cybersecurity breaches globally. In South Africa, credential theft is a major attack vector - attackers buy stolen username and password combinations from data breaches and try them across multiple platforms until something works.

As an IT student, you likely have accounts on your university portal, email, GitHub, LinkedIn, bank apps, and dozens of other platforms. If any one of those uses the same weak password, all of them are at risk.

SA Stat

According to the Fortinet 2024 Cybersecurity Skills Gap Report, weak authentication remains one of the top three causes of breaches in South Africa. And yet most people still use passwords like "Password123" or their date of birth.

The Passphrase Technique

The best passwords are actually passphrases - four or more random words strung together. They are long enough to resist brute force but easy enough to remember.

  • Pick four unrelated words: Sunset · Bicycle · River · Mango
  • Add a separator: Sunset-Bicycle-River-Mango
  • This is 29 characters long and would take modern computers centuries to crack
  • Compare to "P@ssw0rd!" which is only 9 characters and cracked in under an hour
Never Use

Your name, student number, date of birth, "password", "123456", your university name, or any word that appears in a dictionary. These are the first things attackers try.

Topic 2

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Even a strong password can be stolen through phishing or a data breach. MFA adds a second verification step so that even if someone has your password, they still cannot log in without the second factor.

Think of it like your bank card: you need both the card (something you have) and your PIN (something you know). MFA works the same way for online accounts.

🔑
Hardware Key (e.g. YubiKey)
Physical device you plug in or tap. Nearly impossible to phish.
Most Secure
📱
Authenticator App
Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator generates a new 6-digit code every 30 seconds.
Highly Secure
💬
SMS OTP
One-time code sent to your phone. Better than nothing but vulnerable to SIM swap attacks.
Use If No Other Option
👆
Biometrics
Fingerprint or face ID. Convenient and secure for device login.
Good for Devices
SA Warning: SIM Swap Attacks

SIM swap fraud is particularly common in South Africa. Attackers convince your mobile network to transfer your number to their SIM card, then receive your SMS OTPs. This is why authenticator apps are safer than SMS for MFA - your authenticator codes stay on your device, not your phone number.

Enable MFA Now

Go to your Google Account → Security → 2-Step Verification and enable it using Google Authenticator. Also enable it on your UP email, GitHub, LinkedIn, and banking apps. This single step dramatically reduces your risk of account compromise.

Topic 3

Password Managers

You cannot realistically create and remember a unique strong password for every account you have. That is what password managers are for. They store all your passwords securely, encrypted by one master password that only you know.

  • Bitwarden - Free, open-source, and audited. Recommended for students. Available at bitwarden.com
  • 1Password - Paid but excellent. Many companies provide it to employees
  • KeePassXC - Free, open-source, stores everything locally on your device
Have You Been Pwned?

Go to haveibeenpwned.com and enter your email address. This free tool by security researcher Troy Hunt checks if your email and password have appeared in any known data breaches. If it has, change those passwords immediately.
