Introduction

Your Online Presence Has Real Consequences

South African students spend an average of 3 to 4 hours per day on social media. Every post, photo, location tag, and comment contributes to a digital profile that employers, universities, and attackers can access.

This module is about understanding and controlling that profile - protecting yourself legally, professionally, and personally in the South African online environment.

Graduate Job Market Reality

A 2024 survey found that over 70% of South African employers check job applicants' social media profiles before making hiring decisions. What you post today can affect your career in 2026.

Topic 1

Your Digital Footprint

A digital footprint is the trail of data you leave behind whenever you use the internet. It exists in two forms:

Types of Digital Footprint
Active Footprint

Data you intentionally share: posts, photos, comments, profile information, messages you send.

Passive Footprint

Data collected without you actively sharing it: websites visited, location data, browsing history, app usage patterns.

Your footprint is permanent. Even deleted posts may have been screenshotted, cached by Google, or archived. Before posting anything, ask: would I be comfortable if my future employer, my supervisor Prof Eloff, or my family saw this?

Audit Yourself

Google your own name right now. Look at what appears. Check your Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and TikTok profiles from a public (logged out) view. This is what employers see.

Topic 2

Privacy Settings - Practical Checklist

Use this interactive checklist to audit your social media privacy. Click each item when you have completed it.

Your Privacy Audit
Set Instagram account to Private (Settings → Privacy → Account Privacy)
Review Facebook "Who can see your posts" - set to Friends only
Turn off location tagging in photos on Instagram and Facebook
Review and remove third-party apps connected to your social media accounts
Check TikTok privacy settings - set to private if you do not want public content
Remove your full date of birth from Facebook (visible to attackers for identity theft)
Check LinkedIn privacy - make sure your phone number is not publicly visible
Google your name and review what appears publicly
Never Share Publicly

Your ID number, student number, bank account details, physical address, daily routine or commute schedule, photos of your bank card, or anything that could help someone steal your identity or locate you physically.

Topic 3

Cyberbullying and the Law in South Africa

Cyberbullying is not just a social problem in South Africa - it is a criminal one. The Cybercrimes Act of 2021 specifically addresses online harassment and harmful data messages.

  • Online harassment - Repeatedly sending unwanted messages or threats via any digital platform
  • Cyberstalking - Monitoring someone's online activity or location without consent
  • Non-consensual sharing of intimate images - Sharing private sexual images without permission (a serious offence under the Act)
  • Doxxing - Publicly sharing someone's private personal information to cause harm
  • Trolling that causes harm - Repeated targeting of a person with abusive or threatening content
If You Are Being Cyberbullied

Document everything by taking screenshots with timestamps. Block the person. Report to the platform. Report to your university's Student Support Services. If it involves threats, report to SAPS. You do not have to deal with this alone.
