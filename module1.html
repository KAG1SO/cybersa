Introduction

Why This Matters in South Africa

Phishing is the number one cybersecurity threat facing South Africans right now. According to the Interpol 2024 African Cyberthreat Assessment Report, South Africa recorded more phishing attacks than any other country on the continent.

The problem is not just global platforms and foreign hackers. South African attackers are creating locally targeted scams using brands and institutions that South Africans trust every day - SASSA, Standard Bank, FNB, ABSA, Capitec, and even the South African Revenue Service (SARS).

SA Fact

In 2024, South Africa recorded 17,849 ransomware detections - the highest on the African continent. Many of these attacks began with a single phishing email or WhatsApp message.

As a student entering the IT industry, you will be a target. Attackers specifically target graduates applying for jobs, because you are actively sharing your CV, clicking unknown links, and communicating with unfamiliar email addresses.

Topic 1

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of social engineering attack where an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organisation to trick you into revealing sensitive information, clicking a malicious link, or downloading malware.

The word comes from "fishing" - attackers cast a wide net hoping someone takes the bait. The key difference from technical hacking is that phishing attacks the human, not the computer. No advanced technical knowledge is needed to fall victim.

Social Engineering

Social engineering is the psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or giving up confidential information. Phishing is the most common form of social engineering. It exploits trust, urgency, fear, and curiosity - not software vulnerabilities.

Types of Phishing

  • Email Phishing - Fake emails pretending to be from banks, SASSA, or companies. The most common type.
  • Smishing (SMS Phishing) - Fake SMS messages. Very common in SA targeting banking customers.
  • Vishing (Voice Phishing) - Phone calls where attackers pretend to be from your bank or SARS.
  • WhatsApp Phishing - Fake messages sent via WhatsApp, often impersonating known contacts or businesses.
  • Spear Phishing - Targeted phishing using personal information about you to make the attack more convincing.
Topic 2

Recognising Phishing Attempts

Learning to spot phishing is a skill. The more you practise, the better you get. Here are the most important red flags to look for:

Urgency or threats - "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours" or "Act now or lose your SASSA grant"
Suspicious sender address - The display name says "FNB Support" but the email is from fnb-support@gmail-secure.com
Generic greetings - "Dear Customer" instead of your actual name
Suspicious links - Hover over links before clicking. A link that says "fnb.co.za" might actually go to "fnb-secure.xyz"
Requests for personal information - Legitimate organisations never ask for your PIN, password, or OTP via SMS or email
Poor grammar and spelling - Many phishing messages contain obvious errors, though AI is making this less common
Unexpected attachments - Especially .exe, .zip, or Office files you were not expecting
Spot the Phish - Exercise
Which of these two messages is real?
From: ABSA-Bank <security@absa-alerts.co.za>
URGENT: Your ABSA account has been flagged for suspicious activity. Your account will be SUSPENDED in 2 hours unless you verify your details immediately. Click here: http://bit.ly/absa-verify-now

⚠ FAKE - Notice: third-party domain, urgency, bit.ly link, no personalisation

From: ABSA <noreply@absa.co.za>
Hi Kagiso, your ABSA card ending in 4821 was used at Checkers Sandton on 18 Sep 2026 at 14:23 for R245.00. If this was not you, please call 0800 111 155.

✓ REAL - Notice: official domain, your name, specific card details, phone number (no link)

Topic 3

South African Specific Threats

While global phishing tactics apply, South Africa has unique attack vectors that you need to be specifically aware of.

SA Threat 1
SASSA Grant Fraud

Attackers send SMS messages claiming your SASSA Social Relief of Distress (SRD) grant has been approved or there is a problem with your payment. They ask you to click a link and enter your ID number and bank details.

Remember

SASSA will never ask for your banking details or PIN via SMS or WhatsApp. Verify any SASSA communication at sassa.gov.za or call 0800 60 10 11.

SA Threat 2
Fake Graduate Recruitment Emails

Attackers create fake job offers from well-known South African companies like Capitec, KPMG, Deloitte, or government departments. They ask you to fill in a form with your ID number, bank account, and personal details - or to pay a "registration fee" to secure the position.

Remember

Legitimate employers in South Africa never ask for money or your full banking details during the recruitment process. Verify job offers directly on the company's official website.

SA Threat 3
WhatsApp Business Impersonation

Attackers create WhatsApp accounts with the logos of banks or shops and send messages about "unclaimed prizes", "double cashback offers", or "account verification". They may even use a green tick to appear legitimate.

Remember

A green badge on WhatsApp does not guarantee legitimacy. Never share OTPs or banking PINs in any WhatsApp conversation.

Topic 4

Responding and Reporting in South Africa

If you clicked a suspicious link:

  • Do not enter any personal information on the page that opens
  • Close the page immediately and disconnect from Wi-Fi
  • Change your passwords for any accounts that may have been compromised
  • Enable MFA on all accounts if you have not already done so
  • Run a malware scan on your device
  • Contact your bank immediately if you entered any financial details

Where to report phishing in South Africa:

  • Your bank's fraud line - FNB: 087 575 9444 | Standard Bank: 0800 020 600 | ABSA: 0800 111 155 | Capitec: 0860 10 20 43
  • SABRIC (South African Banking Risk Information Centre) - report banking-related phishing at sabric.co.za
  • SAPS Cybercrime Unit - report cybercrime at saps.gov.za or call 10111
  • Cybersecurity Hub - Department of Communications and Digital Technologies: cybersecurityhub.gov.za
  • SASSA fraud line - 0800 60 10 11 if you encounter SASSA-related scams
Quick Tip

Screenshot suspicious messages before deleting them. This creates evidence if you need to file a report with SAPS or your bank.
