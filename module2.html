Introduction

Laws That Govern You Right Now

South Africa now has two major laws that govern how you use the internet, handle other people's data, and behave online. These laws apply to you as an individual - not just to companies.

The curriculum analysis conducted for this research confirmed that none of the seven major South African universities examined teach these laws at the undergraduate level. This means most IT graduates enter the workplace without knowing the legal framework they operate within.

Why This Matters For You

Ignorance of the law is not a defence in South Africa. If you accidentally violate POPIA or the Cybercrimes Act as a new employee, your employer and you personally could face serious consequences.

Topic 1

The Cybercrimes Act of 2021

Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 (Commenced 1 December 2021)
South African Government Gazette | Applies to all individuals and organisations in SA

This Act criminalises a wide range of online activities and creates obligations for reporting cybercrimes. It replaced fragmented older laws with a single comprehensive cybercrime framework.

What the Act Makes Illegal

  • Unlawful access - Accessing a computer system or network without permission, even if you do not steal anything
  • Interception of data - Reading, recording, or redirecting someone else's communications without consent
  • Cyber fraud - Using false online representations to gain financial benefit
  • Cyber forgery - Creating fake digital documents or data
  • Cyber extortion - Demanding money or favours under threat of a cyberattack (e.g. ransomware)
  • Harmful data messages - Sending messages that threaten, harass, or bully someone online
  • Identity-related crimes - Using someone else's personal information to commit a crime
Important: Sharing Passwords

Sharing your university or work login credentials with a classmate so they can access a system is a violation of the Cybercrimes Act. Even if nothing harmful happens, the act of facilitating unauthorised access is illegal.

SA Case Study

In 2021, Transnet, South Africa's state-owned freight and logistics company, was hit by a ransomware attack that shut down its port operations for days. The attackers encrypted Transnet's data and demanded payment. This is a textbook example of cyber extortion under the Cybercrimes Act.

Topic 2

POPIA - Protection of Personal Information Act

Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (Fully effective 1 July 2021)
Enforced by the Information Regulator of South Africa

POPIA governs how personal information must be collected, stored, used, and shared. It gives individuals rights over their own data and places obligations on anyone who handles that data.

What Counts as Personal Information?

  • Your name, ID number, student number, and date of birth
  • Your physical address, email address, and phone number
  • Your biometric data (fingerprints, face recognition)
  • Your financial information including bank account details
  • Your health records and medical history
  • Photos and videos that identify you
  • Your opinions, beliefs, and online behaviour

The Eight POPIA Conditions

Any organisation that processes personal information must comply with all eight conditions:

  • Accountability - The organisation must take responsibility for compliance
  • Processing limitation - Only collect what is necessary for the stated purpose
  • Purpose specification - Be clear about why you are collecting the data
  • Further processing limitation - Do not use data for purposes beyond what was stated
  • Information quality - Keep data accurate and up to date
  • Openness - Be transparent about what data you collect and why
  • Security safeguards - Protect personal data from loss, damage, or unauthorised access
  • Data subject participation - Allow individuals to access and correct their own data
Your Rights Under POPIA

You have the right to know what personal information any organisation holds about you, to request corrections if it is wrong, to object to its use for marketing, and to complain to the Information Regulator if you believe your rights have been violated.

POPIA and Social Media

Posting someone else's personal information on social media without their consent can violate POPIA. This includes sharing screenshots of private conversations, posting someone's ID number, or sharing photos that identify a person without permission.

Topic 3

Consequences of Non-Compliance

15 years
Maximum prison sentence under the Cybercrimes Act for serious offences such as cyber extortion
R10 million
Maximum fine for POPIA non-compliance per violation

The Information Regulator can investigate complaints, issue enforcement notices, and refer matters to the National Prosecuting Authority. Individuals - not just companies - can be held personally liable.

Student Scenario

You build a student app as a side project that collects user email addresses and passwords. You store them in plain text without encryption. If the app is breached and user data is exposed, you could face a POPIA violation as the responsible party - even as a student developer.
