Introduction

South Africa Is Under Attack

South Africa recorded 17,849 ransomware detections in 2024 - the highest on the African continent. The Cisco and University of Pretoria whitepaper found that Africa experiences an average of 2,164 cyberattacks per organisation per week. This is not a distant problem.

As an IT professional, you will encounter these attacks in your career. This module gives you the foundational knowledge to recognise, understand, and respond to the most common attack types you will face in South Africa.

SA Case Study: Transnet 2021

In July 2021, Transnet - South Africa's state freight company - was hit by a ransomware attack that shut down port operations across Durban, Cape Town, and Port Elizabeth for days. The attack cost hundreds of millions of rands and affected the entire SA supply chain. It started with a single compromised credential.

Topic 1

Types of Malware

Malware - short for malicious software - is any software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorised access to a system. Here are the main types you need to know:

🔒 Ransomware
Encrypts your files and demands payment (usually cryptocurrency) to restore access. The Transnet attack was ransomware.
Warning SignsFiles suddenly inaccessible · Pop-up demand for payment · Desktop wallpaper changes to ransom note
👁️ Spyware & Keyloggers
Silently records your keystrokes, screenshots, or browsing activity and sends it to an attacker. Used to steal passwords and banking details.
Warning SignsSlow device · Unknown processes running · Bank account access from unknown locations
🐛 Adware
Displays unwanted ads and may redirect your browser. Often bundled with free software. Less dangerous but a sign of poor security hygiene.
Warning SignsConstant pop-ups · Browser homepage changed · Search engine redirects to unfamiliar sites
🦠 Trojans
Disguises itself as legitimate software. Once installed, it creates a backdoor for attackers. Named after the Trojan horse from Greek mythology.
Warning SignsUnexpected network activity · New programs you did not install · System slowdown after installing software
How Malware Gets In

Email attachments (especially .exe, .zip, and Office files with macros) · Infected USB drives · Fake software downloads from unofficial sites · Visiting compromised websites · Clicking malicious links in messages. Never download software from unofficial sources. Always verify before clicking.

Topic 2

Network Attacks

Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attacks

In a MitM attack, an attacker positions themselves between your device and the server you are communicating with. They can intercept, read, and modify your traffic without either side knowing.

This is especially common on public Wi-Fi networks - at university, coffee shops, shopping centres, and airports. When you connect to "Free_UP_WiFi" without verifying it is legitimate, you could be connecting directly to an attacker's hotspot.

Public Wi-Fi Warning

Never access internet banking, your UP student portal, or any account with sensitive data on public Wi-Fi without a VPN. Attackers on the same network can intercept your credentials in real time.

Denial of Service (DoS and DDoS) Attacks

A DoS attack floods a server or network with so much traffic that it becomes unavailable to legitimate users. A DDoS (Distributed DoS) uses thousands of compromised devices - called a botnet - to amplify the attack.

  • SA government websites including home affairs and SASSA have been targeted by DDoS attacks
  • University systems can be targeted during exam periods
  • ISPs and banks are common targets in South Africa
  • If a service you rely on suddenly becomes unavailable, a DDoS may be the cause
Scenario Exercise
What type of attack is this?
You are working at your laptop in the UP library connected to the campus Wi-Fi. You open your internet banking to check your balance. Later that day you get an SMS from your bank saying your account was accessed from an unknown IP address in Johannesburg. You never left the library.
What most likely happened?
A DDoS attack took down the banking servers
A Man-in-the-Middle attack on the campus Wi-Fi intercepted your banking credentials
Ransomware on your laptop encrypted your banking app
A virus on the library computer spread to your device
Topic 3

Incident Response - What To Do

If you suspect your device or accounts have been compromised, follow these steps in order:

1
Isolate - Disconnect from Wi-Fi and unplug network cables immediately. This prevents the attacker from sending more data or spreading to other devices on your network.
2
Do not turn off - Avoid shutting down completely if ransomware is suspected. A running system preserves evidence in memory that forensic investigators may need.
3
Preserve evidence - Take photos of any ransom notes or error messages. Note the time and what you were doing when the incident started. Do not delete anything.
4
Report to IT - Contact the UP IT Service Desk immediately: 012 420 3111. If this is a workplace incident, contact your IT department and direct manager.
5
Report to authorities - Report to SAPS Cybercrime Unit if financial loss is involved. Contact the Cybersecurity Hub at cybersecurityhub.gov.za for guidance.
6
Do not pay the ransom - Paying does not guarantee you get your files back and funds further attacks. Restore from backup if available.
The Golden Rule

Back up your important files regularly using the 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies of data, on 2 different media types, with 1 copy offsite or in the cloud. If you have a recent backup, a ransomware attack loses most of its power over you.
